# Balance with a fish that can be lost
Generated by `pnpm sim:reel` (web/sim/reel.sim.ts) from the real game rules. It compares two rules for the contracts:
- **Before** (releases up to 004): a lost fight is another try at the same fish, each easier, and the third is hauled in. Every bite ends in the keepnet; skill costs time.
- **After** (release 005 with the reel judge attached): one fight per fish, replayed by the contract. A lost fight is a lost fish: nothing is minted, no XP, no collection or record entry. The energy, the rod wear and the lure use of the cast stay spent. A missed hook is still another bite.
Energy (a bar of 20, one point per 30 minutes), what a cast costs in each water, the odds of each species and what a fish sells for are the same in both. Nothing in this file changes the game.
**Every number below is a simulation.** The three players are models (web/sim/lab.ts, `PLAYERS`), not measurements:
| Player | Reacts after | Reading error | Misses a warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginner | 0.32 s | ±8% of the gauge | 20% |
| Average | 0.25 s | ±6% of the gauge | 10% |
| Experienced | 0.18 s | ±4% of the gauge | 4% |
They hold the needle in the middle of the green and let go when they notice a warning or a jerk. A person who has learned the fight may do better than "Experienced"; a person on a phone with a slow screen may do worse than "Beginner". The telemetry of real fights (#/stats in the game) is what these should be checked against before any number is changed.

## Findings
- **Calm water stays safe.** In the two starting waters a beginner lands 99% and 98% of what bites; the first fights a new player meets (difficulty 0–1) are not lost by any of the three models. What is at stake there is the rare fish: a Legendary in calm water is difficulty 4.
- **Rough water is where fish are lost.** Taimen Rapids is the hardest water: the beginner lands 38% of the bites with the starter rod, the average player 64%, the experienced one 86%. The targets set earlier for a first try in rough water (BALANCE.md) were 45–65% for a novice and 75–90% for an experienced player: the other rough waters are inside or above them, Taimen Rapids is below for the novice.
- **Legendary and Mythic fish.** Outside calm water they all fight at difficulty 5, the cap: with the starter rod the beginner loses 72% of them, the average player 50%, the experienced one 24%. The widest band a rod gives brings that to 54% for the beginner. This is the largest single cost of the rule, and it falls on the catches players care most about.
- **Tackle keeps what it gives.** The green band is computed by the judge from the rod's catch exactly as the page shows it. A better rod is now worth fish, not only seconds: at difficulty 4 the beginner goes from 50% with the starter band to 67% with the widest. The reverse holds too: a specialist rod away from its home water narrows the band and loses more fish than the free rod.
- **PEARL per energy falls by skill.** With the best loadout in each water: 20%–63% for the beginner, 6%–41% for the average player, 1%–15% for the experienced one. Over a year of one sitting a day the PEARL from fish goes 617k → 276k, 628k → 420k, 621k → 539k. Before the change skill bought nothing but time; now it is most of the difference between players.
- **The tackle takes longer to buy**, the last rod most: Pearl Master (150,000 PEARL) on day 136 before, and on day 280 / 203 / 159 after (beginner / average / experienced, one sitting a day).
- **Levels do not come later** in these careers: the model player moves to calm, cheap water, which gives more XP per energy. Somebody who stays in rough water levels more slowly (section 6).
- **Special fish take about twice the energy for a beginner** (section 7). The pity still holds: a special fish that gets away at its pity bites again at once.
- **Upkeep is paid on lost fish too.** Rod wear and lure use belong to the cast. With paid tackle the weaker player therefore loses a larger share of the net than of the fish (section 5).
- **A script does not lose fish.** The fight is decided by a seed anybody can read, so a program can work out a recording that lands it (contracts/test/FightBot.sol does). Before the change everybody landed everything; after it a script earns what "before" shows and people earn what "after" shows. The check proves that the recording lands the fish, not that a person played it.
## Recommendations
None of these is applied. Energy, its regeneration, what a cast costs, the odds and the prices are as they were.
1. **Do not tune before real fights are seen.** The three players are assumptions. The page records every fight in the browser it is played in (difficulty, band, landed / snapped / got away; #/stats, with an export). That log is local and nothing collects it: real rates have to be gathered from testers by hand, and then set against section 1 to see whether people fight like the beginner, the experienced model or neither.
2. **If people fight like the models: soften rough water in the judge, not the economy.** "Gentler rough water" (section 9) brings Taimen Rapids from 38% to about 62% for the beginner and leaves calm water and the rarest fish as they are. It is the judge's `tiers` argument: a new judge deployed and attached over still water, with the same numbers in the client's content, and no upgrade of the game.
3. **Decide whether losing half of the Legendary fish is the game wanted.** For the average player it is one in two outside calm water. If that is too much, "Capped at 4" halves it (one constant in ReelJudge.sol, again a new judge). If it is the point of the game, leave it.
4. **Leave prices and energy alone for now.** Raising what fish pay to make up for lost ones would pay the same rise to players and scripts that lose nothing, and would undo the price pass in BALANCE.md. If progress proves too slow for weak players, the fight is the lever.
5. **Leave upkeep on lost casts.** It is what the rule says (nothing spent on the cast comes back), and refunding wear would need the game, not the judge.

## 10. What did not change
- **The hook.** How fast a hook is set cannot be checked by a contract, so a missed hook is still another bite of the same fish, and the fourth bite has no window. It costs time only: the beginner misses 6% of the hooks at difficulty 0 and 23% at 5. A fish has 10 minutes (600 blocks) from the cast to be landed, which is far more than three missed hooks and a fight of at most 62.4 seconds take.
- **Grades.** The grade of a landed fish (S–D) is still the contract's roll from the cast's seed and the rod's catch. The fight decides whether there is a fish, not how good it is.
- **A clean hook.** Up to release 004 a fast hook started the fight closer to the pier. Under the judge the fight starts from the same place for everybody: a head start would be one more thing the contract cannot check.
- **Energy, odds, prices.** Not touched.
